TOPIC: CONTRACT
1.1 Introduction
Contracts are an important aspect of business, commerce and everyday life because they provide
a systematic approach for exchanging goods, services, promises and agreements.
Almost everyone makes agreements every day, be it individuals, entrepreneurs or organizations.
These agreements are what are known as contracts. A Business Contract is a legally binding
exchange of promises or agreements between parties that the law will enforce. Contracts can
either be written or oral depending on the nature of the agreement, for example, buying a house
requires a written contract whereas lending a K100 to a friend or buying vegetables at the
market may require an oral contract.
This topic will therefore cover what a contract is all about so that learners can demonstrate
knowledge of its formation, its essential elements and discharge of a contract. The topic has one
sub topic and one specific competence with six learning activities.

SUB-TOPIC: BUSINESS CONTRACT
Introduction
It is important for everyone involved in business, weather as an entrepreneur, manager, or
professional to understand the contractual obligations and protect business interests with the
knowledge of contracts.
A business contract is a legally binding agreement between two or more parties that outlines
the terms and conditions for the exchange of goods or services. It is a formal document that
turns promises into detailed lists of rights, rules and obligations that both parties must follow.
Business contracts play a crucial role in ensuring that business dealings are conducted smoothly,
efficiently and fairly. A business contract helps establish clear guidelines, reduces the chance
of disputes, protects legal rights, and provides a framework for resolving issues if they arise.

Content Tips
A business contract is extremely important for the following reasons:
 Clarity and Expectations: A contract clearly defines the terms of the agreement
between parties, including deliverables, timelines, responsibilities, payment terms,
and more. This helps to ensure that everyone involved understands their role and
what is expected.
 Legal Protection: A well-drafted contract provides legal protection in case of
disputes. If one party does not fulfill their obligations, the other party can refer to
the contract and, if necessary, take legal action to enforce the agreement or seek
damages.
 Risk Management: Contracts help to outline the risks involved in a business
relationship and set out the processes for addressing potential issues, such as non-
performance, breaches of terms, or unforeseen events. This helps mitigate risk and
ensures both parties are prepared.
 Prevents Misunderstandings: By putting the terms in writing, a contract helps
prevent misunderstandings and miscommunications between the parties. It can
serve as a reference point to resolve any disagreements.
 Regulates Payment Terms: Contracts specify the payment terms, which help
businesses manage their cash flow. This includes the amount, timing, and method
of payment, which ensures both parties know when and how payments will be
made.
 Dispute Resolution: Contracts often contain clauses specifying how disputes will
be resolved—whether through mediation, arbitration, or litigation. This can save
time and money in resolving conflicts without going to court.
 Establishing Professionalism and Trust: A contract helps to establish trust and
professionalism between businesses. It signals a commitment to doing business
properly and can make all parties feel more secure in their dealings.
 Compliance with Laws: In some cases, contracts are necessary for compliance
with regulatory or industry-specific laws. Having a written agreement ensures that
businesses are meeting legal requirements.

Content Tips
1. Offer
 An offer is an expression of willingness to contract on certain terms with the
intention that as soon as it is accepted it becomes binding.
 One party makes an offer and another party accepts the offer to complete the
bargain i.e. ‘meeting of the minds’ of the parties.
Lapse of an Offer
An offer comes to an end by:
 Revocation where the Offeror revokes the offer at any time before it is accepted
 Lapse of time set in the offer
 Death or Insanity of either the offeror or the offeree
 Rejection where the offeree rejects the offer
 Counter offer where by the offeree makes a counter offer other than the original
offer.
 Invitation to Treat
 It is the preliminary step in the contracting process where one party invites
others to make an offer.
2. Acceptance
Acceptance is the final and complete expression of agreement to the terms of an
offer.
Acceptance of an offer may be by Signing of a contract or by performance of the
requested act.
It is no defense to an action based on a contract for the defendant to claim that he
never intended to be bound by the agreement if under all the circumstances it is
shown at trial that his conduct was such that it communicated to the other party or
parties that the defendant had in fact agreed. Acceptance must be communicated to
the offeror. The following must be observed:
 An offer can only be accepted by the offeree, that is, the person to whom the
offer is made.

Teaching Module7
 An offeree is not bound if another person accepts the offer on his behalf without
his authorization
 If the offer specifies the method of acceptance, you must accept it using a method
that is no less effective than the method specified.
 Silence cannot be taken as acceptance
3. Consideration
Consideration is anything of value (an item or service) that each party to a contract
agrees to exchange if the contract is to be valid. It is a requirement that for parties to
be able to enforce a promise they must have given something in return. The idea is
that both parties to a contract must bring something to the bargain.
The rules governing consideration are:
 Consideration must be sufficient, but should not be inadequate. It is not for the
courts to compare values. As long as the parties have agreed the contract is valid.
 Consideration must not be from the past – consideration must be provided at
the time the contract is being created. Contracts that rest on past consideration
(consideration that comes after agreement has been made) are not enforceable
 Consideration must move from the offeree – for instance, it is not good
consideration for person A to pay person C in return for services rendered by
person B.
4. Intention to be legally bound
There is a presumption for commercial agreements that parties intend to be legally
bound. On the other hand, many kinds of domestic and social agreements are
unenforceable, for instance between children and parents. Hence it is true to say all
contracts are agreements but not all agreements are contracts.
5. Capacity
This is the legal ability to enter into a contract. However, the following have no
capacity:
 The Minor - this is someone below the age of 18. The only contract a minor
may be bound is that of necessaries including Contracts of education which are
for the minors benefits in life.
 The Insane/Drunken Person - These are people who are mentally incapacitated,
either by disability or drunkenness. Contracts made by these people may not be
enforceable.
 Also note that when the law limits or bars a person from engaging in specified
activities, any agreement or contracts with such a person is either voidable or
void for incapacity.
6. Legality
A contract is void if it is based on an illegal purpose or contrary to public policy. For
a contract to be valid the subject matter must be legal. For instance, a contract to
steal someone else’s car is void.

Content Tips
The discharge of a business contract refers to the process by which the contractual
obligations of the parties involved are ended or fulfilled, releasing them from further
duties under the agreement. Discharge of a contract can occur in several ways, and
it’s important for learners to understand how these methods work to ensure legal
compliance and avoid disputes. Here are the main ways a business contract can be
discharged (come to an end):
1. Performance
 Complete Performance: A contract is typically discharged when both parties
fulfill their obligations as outlined in the agreement. If all the terms and
conditions of the contract are met (e.g., payment is made, goods are delivered,
services are rendered), the contract is considered discharged by performance.

Form 1 Term 3
Teaching Module11
 Partial Performance: Sometimes, a party may partially perform their obligations
but not fully meet the terms of the contract. In such cases, the contract may not
be fully discharged unless the other party accepts the partial performance. In
some situations, the non-performing party may be liable for damages or breach
of contract.
2. Agreement (Mutual Agreement)
 Rescission: Both parties may agree to cancel or “rescind” the contract. This
discharges the obligations under the contract, and both parties are generally
returned to their original positions (if possible).
 When one of the original parties to the contract is replaced by a new party, with
the consent of all involved. A new contract replaces the old one, and the old
party is discharged from further obligations.
 Modification or Alteration: If both parties agree to change the terms of the
contract (such as price, timeline, scope, etc.), the contract can be discharged and
replaced by a modified version. This is common in long-term contracts.
3. Breach of Contract
 If one party indicates that they will not or cannot perform their obligations under
the contract (either explicitly or implicitly), this is considered a breach. The
non-breaching party has the right to discharge the contract and may also seek
damages.
 When one party announces, before the performance is due, that they will
not fulfill their part of the contract. The other party can treat the contract as
discharged and seek compensation for the breach immediately.
 If one party fails to perform the contract as agreed (e.g., failing to deliver goods
or services), the contract can be discharged, and the aggrieved party may seek
remedies, including damages or specific performance.
4. Frustration
 A contract may be discharged due to frustration if unforeseen events make
performance impossible or radically different from what was initially agreed
upon. This could include things like the destruction of the subject matter, a
change in the law that makes performance illegal, or a natural disaster that makes
performance impossible. In such cases, the contract is automatically discharged,
and neither party is typically liable for damages.
5. Impossibility of Performance
 This is similar to frustration but is more specific to cases where the performance
of the contract becomes physically or legally impossible. For example, if a
key supplier of materials goes out of business, or a major piece of equipment
required for a service is destroyed in a fire, the contract may be discharged
because it is no longer possible to perform the agreed-upon actions.
6. Operation of Law

 Bankruptcy: If one party to the contract is declared bankrupt or is insolvent,
the contract may be discharged through the legal process of bankruptcy. In
such cases, the business is no longer legally obligated to fulfill its contractual
obligations.
 Death or Incapacity or insanity: If a contract involves personal services or
skills (such as a freelance contractor), the death or incapacity of the person
involved may discharge the contract. If the services cannot be rendered by the
individual, the contract may be discharged by operation of law.
 Time Limitation (Statute of Limitations): If a claim under a contract is not
brought within a legally prescribed time period (as defined by the statute of
limitations), the right to enforce the contract is discharged, and the claim can no
longer be pursued in court.
 Vitiating Factors: these refer to circumstances or elements that can nullify or
render a contract voidable (changeable). These factors can make a contract
unenforceable or allow one or both parties to escape their obligations. Example
of vitiating factors; duress, undue influence, misrepresentation, fraud, mistakes,
illegality and capacity.
Consequences of Discharge:
 Once a contract is discharged, the parties are released from their future
obligations, although some obligations (such as confidentiality or indemnity
clauses) may survive the termination of the contract.
 If the contract is discharged due to breach or frustration, the party responsible
for the discharge (often the breaching party) may be liable for damages or other
legal remedies.

Entrepreneurship is the process of designing, launching and running a new business. It’s the
ability and willingness to develop, organize and manage a business along with all the risks
