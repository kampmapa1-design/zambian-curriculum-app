 It involves distribution and organisation of human settlements.
 There are three major patterns of settlements namely; dispersed/isolated, nucleated/

 Dispersed or isolated settlement pattern is where individual buildings or households

settlements.
 Nucleated or clustered settlements comprise of houses that are close to each other.
Usually houses cluster or concentrated around something e. g road junction or
source of water.
 Linear or Ribbon settlements comprise of settlement patterns where buildings or
houses are in a form of a line usually along features such as roads, rivers or streams,
railways etc.

The location of settlements is one of the crucial aspect in the study of human adaptation as it
influences the development, growth and sustainability of communities. Settlements have been

the complexities of human habitation, economic development and environmental sustainability.

1.2. TOPIC 2: URBANISATION
Introduction
Urbanisation is the process of people moving from rural areas to cities and urban centres, it
is the global phenomenon, with increasing numbers of people living in urban areas. Zambia’s
urban population is growing rapidly with significant implications for economies, societies, and
environments. The country has experienced significant urbanisation in recent decades, driven
by physical, social and economic factors. Major cities like Lusaka, Kitwe, and Ndola are hubs of
economic activities, innovation, and cultural diversity.
Urbanisation in Zambia presents both opportunities for economic growth and development, as
well as challenges such as housing shortages, infrastructure strain, and environmental degradation.
Understanding urbanisation in Zambia is crucial for addressing the country’s development
challenges and promoting sustainable urban growth. This topic will explore the relationship
between urbanisation, daily lives and the environment
Hook: ‘Imagine waking up one morning to find your village transformed into a busting
city! What would your life be like? Would you love the challenges or miss the old ways?’
