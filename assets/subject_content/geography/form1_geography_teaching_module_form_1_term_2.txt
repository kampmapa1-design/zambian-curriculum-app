1.1. TOPIC 1: NATURAL RESOURCES
Introduction
Natural resources are materials like water, air, trees, and minerals that come from nature and are
essential for life. Some of these such as sunlight and forests, can be replaced over time, while
others, such as oil and minerals, take millions of years to form and could run out. Responsible
use of natural resources is important to ensure they last. This includes avoiding waste, protecting
the environment, and using resources wisely. As Zambia’s population grows, more resources are
needed for food, energy, and development. This creates pressure on natural resources, so careful
management is necessary to balance today’s needs with future sustainability. Managing natural
resources involves actions like replanting trees, protecting water sources, and reducing
pollution. By doing this, we can support both the people of today and future generations while
keeping the environment healthy.
This topic will explore responsible use of natural resources, the relationship between population
and these resources and sustainable management practices that ensure their preservation for
future generations while meeting the needs of the present.
Hook: ‘Have you wondered where your desk, clothes and the food you eat (sandwich,
nshima, bread) come from? The answer lies in something we often take for granted:
natural resources’

1.1.2. SUB-TOPIC 2: POPULATION AND NATURAL RESOURCES
Introduction
The relationship between population and natural resources is a critical element in understanding
the dynamics of human development and environmental sustainability. As the Zambian population
continues to grow, the demand for essential resources such as water, land, energy, and minerals
increases exponentially. Natural resources are the foundation of human survival and economic
activity, providing the raw materials for food, shelter, industry, and energy. However, these
resources are finite, and their overexploitation can lead to environmental degradation, scarcity,
and conflict.
Population growth amplifies pressure on ecosystems, often leading to unsustainable practices
in agriculture, mining, deforestation, and energy consumption. In densely populated regions,
the strain on natural resources can outpace the Earth’s capacity to regenerate them, resulting in
pollution, habitat loss, and a decline in biodiversity.
Specific competence – Learners to:
Analyze the relationship between population and natural resources.

1.1.3. SUB-TOPIC 3: SUSTAINABLE MANAGEMENT OF NATURAL RESOURCE.
Introduction:
Sustainable management means using natural resources in a careful and responsible way so that
they last for a long time. Sustainable management of natural resources aims to balance human
needs with environmental protection, ensuring that these resources are available for current
and future generations. This approach promotes responsible use, conservation, and restoration
strategies that align with ecological principles and long-term sustainability goals. By integrating
environmental, social, and economic considerations, sustainable resource management plays a
crucial role in building a more resilient and equitable world.

Content Tips
There are many causes of migration. The causes are divided into push and pull factors.
 Push factors are those things that force people to migrate while pull factors are those
that attract people to settle in different areas.
 Employment Opportunities: People move to areas with better job prospects, such as
cities or foreign countries.
 Access to Social Services: Migration occurs when people seek better healthcare,
education, and housing.
 Recreational and Urban Facilities: Cities attract migrants with entertainment centers,
modern infrastructure, and advanced services.
 Conflicts and Disputes: Wars, political instability, and ethnic conflicts force people
to migrate for safety.
 Natural Disasters: Floods, droughts, and other disasters push people to safer areas.
 Food Insecurity: When agricultural production is low, people migrate to regions

 Understanding population distribution is crucial for effective planning, resource

infrastructure development.

 Zambia’s rapid population growth increase has led to both challenges and
opportunities.
 The country’s rapid population growth has been caused by high birth rates, early

utilization of natural resources, and improved security.
