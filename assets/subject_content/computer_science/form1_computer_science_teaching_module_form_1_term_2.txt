The introduction of the competence-based curriculum (CBC) marks a transformative step in
improving education quality and relevance. This Form 1 Computer Science Teaching Module
has been meticulously designed to support teachers in effectively delivering Computer Science
lessons under the new curriculum. The module aims to bridge the gap created by the absence
of approved textbooks and to equip educators with practical tools, activities, and assessments
tailored to the learners’ level and needs.
As a Ministry, we understand the challenges that come with transitioning to a new curriculum
framework. This module reflects our commitment to making this journey as smooth as possible
for teachers, by providing structured guidance, suggested activities, and formative assessments
that align with the CBC’s objectives. However, we also encourage educators to supplement this
module with their own research and innovations to address any gaps that may arise during
implementation.
The suggested activities and assessments provided in this module are designed to foster practical
and critical thinking skills in learners while ensuring relevance and adaptability to different school
contexts. Teachers are encouraged to tailor these suggestions to their unique classroom dynamics
and to explore alternative, acceptable approaches when necessary. This flexibility is key to
ensuring that all learners, regardless of their environment, benefit from meaningful and impactful
Computer Science education.
We hope this module will serve as a valuable resource for teachers as they navigate the new
competence-based curriculum, enhancing their ability to inspire and equip learners with essential
Computer Science knowledge and skills. Together, let us work towards creating a vibrant and
responsive education system that prepares our learners for the demands of the digital age.

Guidelines for Teachers on Using Computer Science Module
The Computer Science modules for Forms 1 to 4 are designed to equip learners with foundational
knowledge of how computers work, programming skills, and problem-solving abilities. In the
absence of approved textbooks, these modules will serve as comprehensive teaching resources
for delivering impactful Computer Science lessons.
1. Understand the Nature of Computer Science as a Subject
Computer Science focuses on:
• The science behind computers, including understanding hardware, software, and networks.
• Developing computational thinking skills to analyze and solve problems.
• Writing and debugging programs in programming languages like Python or Scratch.
• Exploring how data is stored, processed, and managed.
As a science, it goes beyond just using tools—it explains how those tools work and
empowers learners to create solutions.

3. Teach Programming Through Practice
Programming is a hands-on skill that requires regular practice. To teach programming:
• Use the modules to introduce programming fundamentals, such as variables, loops, and
decision-making structures.
• Guide learners to write simple programs, such as a calculator, using the provided examples
in the modules.
• Introduce platforms like Python, Scratch, or Code.org for interactive coding sessions.
• Encourage learners to debug their code and explore why errors occur. This reinforces
problem-solving skills.
4. Use Examples and Exercises to Explain Concepts
The modules contain worked examples and exercises to simplify complex concepts. Teachers
should:
• Guide learners through each example, explaining the logic behind the solution. For instance,
demonstrate how a loop works by writing a program that counts from 1 to 10.
• Assign practical exercises from the modules, such as creating algorithms or designing
flowcharts.
• Relate programming concepts to real-life applications, such as creating a simple weather
app or automating a task.
5. Relate Concepts to Real-World Applications
Learners grasp concepts better when they understand their relevance. For example:
• Explain how databases are used in everyday life, such as managing inventory in a store.
• Discuss how programming is used to develop games, apps, or websites.
• Highlight how artificial intelligence and robotics rely on programming skills.
6. Foster Collaboration and Problem-Solving
Collaborative learning builds confidence and teamwork. Teachers can:
• Organize coding challenges where learners solve problems in pairs or groups.
• Assign group projects, such as designing a game or creating a data management program.
• Encourage learners to share their approaches to problem-solving and learn from one
another.
7. Focus on Assessment and Feedback
Assessment in Computer Science should test both theoretical understanding and practical skills.
Teachers should:
• Use quizzes in the modules to evaluate learners’ knowledge of hardware, software, and
algorithms.
• Assign coding tasks and grade learners based on functionality, efficiency, and creativity of
their programs.
• Provide constructive feedback, pointing out errors in logic and suggesting improvements.

This topic introduces learners to the fundamentals of cybersecurity and the importance of
safeguarding digital systems and personal data. It covers common threats such as hacking,
malware, phishing, and ransomware, highlighting the impact of cyber-attacks on individuals

digital environment.

Content Tips
• Definition:
Transforming raw data into meaningful, usable information.
• Stages of Data Processing:
o Data Collection: Gathering raw data from various sources.
o Data Cleaning (Preparation): Removing errors, duplicates, and inconsistencies.
o Data Input: Converting cleaned data into a machine-readable format.
o Data Processing: Applying algorithms, calculations, or transformations to the data.
o Data Storage: Saving processed data for easy retrieval and future use.
o Data Output: Presenting the results in a readable form (e.g., reports, charts).
o Data Interpretation: Analyzing the output to support decision-making.
• Methods of Data Processing:
o Manual Processing: Done by humans without automation (slow and error-prone).
o Mechanical Processing: Using simple machines (e.g., calculators, typewriters).
o Electronic Processing: Using computers and software (fast and efficient).
• Types of Data Processing:
o Batch Processing: Handling large volumes of data at once (e.g., payroll systems).
o Real-Time Processing: Instant processing as data comes in (e.g., ATM transactions).
o Online Processing: Data processed immediately with user interaction (e.g.,
e-commerce).
o Distributed Processing: Data processed across multiple computers or servers.
• Importance of Data Processing:
o Enhances decision-making.
o Improves efficiency and accuracy.
o Enables large-scale data analysis (Big Data, AI …).
