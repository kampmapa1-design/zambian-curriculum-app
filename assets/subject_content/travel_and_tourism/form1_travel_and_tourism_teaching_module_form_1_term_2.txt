This Travel and Tourism Competence Based Teachers’ Module is a learners’ gateway to
exploring one of the most dynamic and ever-evolving industries globally. The subject of Travel and
tourism is not just about visiting new places; it encompasses the excitement of discovering diverse
cultures, the excitement of adventure, and the joy of creating unforgettable memories.
Throughout this module, teachers will delve into the core principles of travel and tourism, learning
about various destinations, the history of travel, and the essential skills needed to navigate this
industry. They will explore the impact of tourism on economies, cultures, and environments, and
understand the importance of sustainable travel practices. Practical activities and real-world examples
will help learners apply theoretical knowledge, making the concepts more relatable and easier to
grasp.
This module aims to build learners’ competences in the field of travel and tourism, equipping them
with the skills to plan and manage travel experiences, understand customer needs, and provide
excellent service. They will gain insight into the details of the tourism industry, from tour planning
and hospitality management to marketing and customer relations. Additionally, they will learn the
importance of cultural sensitivity, ethical practices, and environmental stewardship, all crucial
elements in promoting responsible tourism.
This teaching module has equally integrated some cross cutting issues such as financial education,
entrepreneurship, environmental health, and waste management.
The learners of Travel and Tourism will develop a deeper appreciation for the art of travel and the
important role it plays in connecting people and places. Their newfound knowledge will empower
them to pursue a career in travel and tourism with confidence and enthusiasm, making them not just
learners, but future leaders in the industry.

Assessment Methods
Assessment methods in competence-based Travel and Tourism lessons should focus on evaluating
learners' skills, knowledge, and ability to apply what they have learned. These methods ensure that
learners are evaluated in a way that highlights their abilities to apply knowledge effectively and
confidently in real-world situations.
The following are some effective methods:
1. Practical Demonstrations: Learners can carry out various tasks. Teachers observe and evaluate
their techniques, practices and ability to follow instructions.
2. Role-Playing: Learners act out different scenarios. This helps assess their problem-solving and
communication skills.
3. Project-Based Assessment: Assign projects such as brochure creating, creating an itinerary and
survey designing. This evaluates their creativity, teamwork, and organisational skills.
4. Written Assessments: Use quizzes, short-answer tests, or worksheets to check learners'

TOPIC 1.3: MOTIVATION IN TOURISM
Introduction
Motivation in Tourism is a fundamental concept that drives individuals to engage in travel. Whether
it’s for relaxation, adventure, learning, or cultural exploration, understanding the reasons behind
travel helps the tourism industry to cater to diverse needs. Motivation can vary greatly from person
to person and can be influenced by a multitude of factors ranging from personal desires to external
circumstances. In tourism, motivation often explains why people choose certain destinations,
activities, or experiences, and it directly impacts the success of tourism businesses.
Understanding what motivates people to travel is essential for businesses, governments, and
organizations involved in tourism, as it helps in designing better travel experiences, services, and
marketing strategies. By analysing travel motivations, the tourism sector can ensure that they meet
the expectations and desires of tourists, leading to satisfaction and repeat visits.
General Competence(s)
Learners should: Acquire the following General Competence(s):
• Analytical Thinking : Analysing factors influencing travel and how they apply in
real-world scenarios
• Digital Literacy : Use digital platforms (websites, social media) to reach
destinations and engage with customers.
• Critical thinking : Analyse a real-world situation and evaluate travel
motivators, using logical reasoning to select appropriate
destinations.
• Creativity and Innovation: Designing brochures or infographics with persuasive
elements express.
• Problem solving : Applying knowledge to select destinations based on travel
Motivators.
Hook:
Question: ask learners “Why do people travel to different places? What would make you want to
visit a new place?” or
Ask learners “Imagine you are planning your next vacation. What factors would influence your
decision on where to go and what to do? Is it the weather? The culture? Or maybe your friends or
family’s recommendations? Think about it for a moment, and let’s d ive into the factors that make us
pack our bags and set off on an adventure!

Key Terms
Key word Description
Adventure Travel A type of travel motivated by the desire for excitement, physical
challenges, and outdoor activities in natural environments, often
involving risk and exploration.
Cultural Motivators These motivations arise from the desire to experience different cultures,
traditions, and customs. This can include activities like visiting historical
sites, museums, or festivals.
Interpersonal
Motivators
The social reasons for travel, such as visiting family and friends,
attending social gatherings, or participating in group travel experiences.
Physical Motivators These are motivations related to physical well-being, such as the desire
for relaxation, escape, or adventure activities like hiking, swimming, or
mountaineering.
Pull Factors The factors that attract individuals to a specific destination, such as
natural beauty, cultural heritage, or a reputation for adventure tourism.
These are the characteristics of a destination that attract travelers.
Push Factors The factors that motivate individuals to leave their home environment,
such as the need for relaxation, escape, or adventure. These factors are
often psychological in nature.
Travel Motivation The reasons or factors that drive an individual to travel. These
motivations can be physical, psychological, social, cultural, or
environmental, influencing the decision to visit a particular destination.
Status and Prestige
Motivators
These motivations are driven by the desire to enhance one's social
standing or reputation, often by visiting luxurious or exclusive
destinations or partaking in high-status activities.
Survey A research method used to collect data from people, often in the form of
questions, to understand preferences, behaviors, or opinions. In this
context, it is used to understand what motivates people to engage in
adventure travel.

Sub-Topic 1.3.1: Motivation to Travel
Introduction
This topic introduces learners to the concept of travel motivation; the reasons why people travel and
the factors that influence their decisions. It explores different types of motivators, activities that
encourage travel, and how to analyse travel trends using a simple survey. Understanding travel
motivation helps learners appreciate the tourism industry and its impact on individuals and
communities.

Learning environment: Indoor or Outdoor (Natural/Artificial/Technological)

Factors Influencing Travel Motivation
1. Income (Financial Factors)
• The amount of money a person has affects their ability to travel.
• High-income earners can afford flights, luxury hotels, and expensive activities.
• Low-income earners may travel less or choose budget-friendly destinations.
4

Example:
• A wealthy tourist from Europe may visit Zambia and stay at a luxury lodge in South Luangwa.
• A learner with a low budget may travel locally for a school trip instead.
2. Climate (Weather Conditions)
• Climate plays an important role in travel decisions.
• Tourists prefer destinations with comfortable weather.
• Some travel to escape extreme weather (hot summers or cold winters).
Example:
• People from cold European countries travel to warm beach destinations in winter.
• Tourists avoid areas with frequent hurricanes, floods, or extreme heat.
How does this apply to Zambia?
• Zambia’s warm and dry season attracts tourists for safaris and Victoria Falls tours.
• The rainy season may reduce tourist visits to national parks.
3. Safety (Health and Crime Risks)
• Tourists avoid destinations where they feel unsafe.
• Countries with low crime rates attract more visitors.
• Health concerns (such as disease outbreaks) can discourage travel.
Example:
• Tourists avoid places with high crime rates or political instability.
• During the COVID-19 pandemic, many people canceled international trips due to health risks.
How does this apply to Zambia?
• Zambia is considered a safe country for tourists, making it attractive.
• Proper health measures in hotels and parks help maintain tourism.
4. Security (Government Protection and Stability)
• Security involves government policies and law enforcement to protect tourists.
• Strong security measures in airports, hotels, and tourist sites increase confidence.
• Countries with terrorism or war attract fewer tourists.
Example:
• Countries with strict airport security (like the USA) make tourists feel safe.
• Tourists avoid war-torn countries like Syria or Afghanistan.
How does this apply to Zambia?
• Zambia’s political stability and peaceful environment attract international visitors.
• Tourists feel safe in destinations like Livingstone and Lusaka.
5

5. People’s Hospitality (Friendliness and Customer Service)
• Tourists enjoy destinations where locals are friendly, welcoming, and helpful.
• Good customer service in hotels, restaurants, and tourist attractions improves experiences.
• Bad hospitality (rudeness, discrimination) can discourage tourists.
Example:
• Tourists love Thailand because of the warm and friendly hospitality.
• Some visitors complain about unfriendly treatment in certain destinations.
How does this apply to Zambia?
• Zambians are known for their warm hospitality, making the country attractive to visitors.
• Training hotel and tour staff in good customer service enhances tourism.

Main Types of Travel Motivators:
1. Physical Motivators
These are related to the physical well-being and relaxation of an individual. They include:
• Rest and relaxation: People travel to take a break from work or stress.
• Health and medical purposes: Some people visit health resorts, spas, or wellness centers.
• Sports and adventure: Engaging in activities such as hiking, skiing, or scuba diving.
• Climate and environment: Traveling to places with a favorable climate (e.g., from cold to
warm regions).
10

2. Cultural Motivators
These are linked to the desire to explore and learn about different cultures. Examples include:
• Historical and heritage tourism: Visiting historical sites, museums, and ancient ruins.
• Art and music: Attending festivals, concerts, and art exhibitions.
• Religious tourism: Pilgrimages to religious sites like Mecca, the Vatican, or Jerusalem.
• Education and learning: Traveling to study or attend cultural events.
3. Interpersonal Motivators
These involve social connections and the need to interact with others. Examples include:
• Visiting friends and relatives: Many people travel to reunite with family and friends.
• Social networking: Meeting new people through tourism activities.
• Celebrations and reunions: Attending weddings, birthdays, or special occasions.
4. Status and Prestige Motivators
These motivators are related to personal achievements and social status. They include:
• Luxury travel: Staying in high-end hotels and traveling first class.
• Exclusive experiences: Visiting unique or remote destinations.
• Business and professional reasons: Attending international conferences or high-level
meetings.
• Personal fulfillment: Participating in volunteer tourism or adventure challenges.
Activity 4: Designing a simple survey: asking people in their community or family
about their motivations for adventure travel
(present their findings in a
report, discussing common trends in adventure travel)
Discussion
Step 1: Introduction & Discussion
• Begin by asking the class: "What is adventure travel?"
- Guide learners to understand that adventure travel includes activities like hiking, safaris, zip-
lining, mountain climbing, and water rafting.
• Discuss why people engage in adventure travel (thrill-seeking, fitness, exploring nature,
social bonding…).
Step 2: Designing the Survey
• Learners to form small groups (3-5 Learners per group).
• Brainstorm survey questions to understand why people in their community engage in
adventure travel.
11

• Provide a template with 5-7 key questions for their survey, such as:
1. Have you ever gone on an adventure trip? If yes, where?
2. What motivated you to go on this trip? (fun, fitness, social reasons…)
3. What type of adventure travel interests you most?
4. Do you prefer local or international adventure trips? Why?
5. What challenges do people face when planning adventure trips?
• Competencies Developed: Research, Critical Thinking, Question Formulation
Step 3: Conducting the Survey (Homework or Weekend Task)
• Learners to interview 5–10 people in their family or community using their survey
questions.
• Learners collect responses through word of the mouth, face-to-face interviews, phone
calls, or written forms.
• Encourage learners to take notes or record key responses.
• Competencies Developed: Data Collection, Communication, Active Listening
Step 4: Analysing the Findings
• Back in class, groups organise and categorise their data (listing most common
motivators).
• Learners to identify trends (“Most people travel for relaxation and fun” or “Few people
engage in adventure travel due to high costs” …).
• Each group writes a short summary of their findings.
• Competencies Developed: Data Interpretation, Problem-Solving, Analytical Thinking
Step 5: Writing the Report
Each group writes a structured report with the following sections:
1. Introduction : Explaining the purpose of the survey.
2. Survey Method : How and where they collected data.
3. Findings : What the data revealed about adventure travel motivations.
4. Conclusion : Summary of key trends.
5. Recommendations : How to encourage more adventure travel.
• Competencies Developed: Writing Skills, Summarizing, Structuring Reports
Step 6: Presenting Findings
• Each group presents their report in front of the class using a poster, PowerPoint, or verbal
summary.
• The teacher facilitates a class discussion on common patterns across all groups.
• Competencies Developed: Public Speaking, Presentation Skills, Teamwork
Expected Standard: Aspects of travel motivation analysed correctly
