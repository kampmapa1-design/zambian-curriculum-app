The Form 1 Teaching Module has been meticulously designed to support teachers in the effective delivery
of English Language lessons under the reformed curriculum. The module is developed based on the
approved 2024 Competence-Based English Language Syllabus. It aims to bridge the gap created by the
absence of approved textbooks and to equip educators with practical tools, activities, and assessments
tailored to the learners’ level and needs.
The Ministry, understands the challenges that come with transitioning to a new curriculum that is
Competence Based. The choice of the Competence-Based Curriculum (CBC) marks a transformative
step in improving education quality and relevance. This module reflects the Ministry’s unwavering
commitment to making this journey as smooth as possible for teachers, by providing structured guidance,
suggested activities, and formative assessments that align with the CBC’s objectives. However, teachers
are encouraged to supplement this module with their own research and innovations in order to address
any gaps that may arise during implementation.
The suggested activities and assessments provided in this module are designed to foster practical and
critical thinking skills in learners while ensuring relevance and adaptability to different school contexts.
Teachers are encouraged to tailor these suggestions to their unique classroom dynamics and to explore
alternative, acceptable approaches when necessary. This flexibility is key to ensuring that all learners,
regardless of their environment, benefit from meaningful and impactful Curriculum.
It is envisaged that this module will serve as a valuable resource for teachers as they navigate the
new Competence-Based Curriculum, enhancing their ability to inspire and equip learners with essential
English Language knowledge, skills and positive attitudes desired to create a vibrant and responsive
education system.

INTRODUCTION
Overview: Public speaking is the art or skill of delivering a speech or presentation to an audience. It
can take place in many settings like a classroom, at a conference, a business meeting or a social
gathering. Public speaking is about communicating ideas effectively. Apart from words, body
language, vocal tone and audience engagement are also essential for effective public speaking. This
topic will cover debate.
General Competence(s):
❑ Communication: Using appropriate language (verbal and non-verbal) in different situations.

KEY TERMS /WORDS/VOCABULARY
❑ Audience: the group of people you are speaking to
❑ Tone: the emotion and emphasis behind the speaker’s voice
❑ Voice Projection: speaking loudly and clearly so that everyone can hear
❑ Persuasion: the ability to convince or influence an audience
❑ Eye Contact: the act of looking at the audience to engage them and establish connection
❑ Body Language: the non-verbal gestures, posture, and movements that communicate a
message
SUB-TOPIC – DEBATE

persuasive speaking, and logical reasoning. Debates are common in education, politics and
competitions to explore different views on important issues. This topic aims at equipping learners
with skills to use in carrying out a debate.

1. Stress is the body’s natural, human response to challenges or demands. It is a feeling of
being under pressure, overwhelmed, or unable to cope with certain situations. Many
different situations can cause stress, and this can change depending on our age. For

situations. In comparison, young and older adults may struggle with stress relating to

2. Stress in relationships can affect the way you think, feel, and behave. Stress in

INTRODUCTION
Overview: Narrative writing is a type of composition that tells a story with a clear beginning, middle
and end. It involves presenting a sequence of events, either real or imagined, in a way that engages
the reader. It usually has characters who drive the action. Narratives can be written in different ways
with characters as the main movers of the action. A strong narrative engages the audience by
creating vivid imagery and emotional depth. It can be fiction or non-fiction. In this topic we are going
to focus on story writing.

INTRODUCTION
Overview: Result is used to express the outcome or effect of a particular cause or action. The
structure helps us to connect actions to their consequences.
General Competence(s)
❑ Communication: Express oneself (spoken and written) clearly and effectively
 Use appropriate language in different situations
Hook: Every action we take leads to an outcome. What language can help us to express how one
event triggers another?
KEY TERMS /WORDS/VOCABULARY
❑ Cause and effect: the relationship between an action (cause) and its consequence (effect)
❑ Outcome: The end result or effect of an action
❑ Consequence: a result, especially one that follows an action naturally or logically
SUBTOPIC: DIFFERENT WAYS OF EXPRESSING RESULT
INTRODUCTION
When we want to express result in a sentence, we use certain structures to show cause and effect
relationships. In such constructions, an action or situation is connected to its result. This lesson
explores different ways of expressing result.
SPECIFIC COMPETENCE: Use different expressions of result in sentences
LEARNING ACTIVITIES
ACTIVITY 1: DISCUSSING DIFFERENT EXPRESSIONS OF RESULT
Type 1: Using Therefore, as a result, for this reason, consequently, to express result
❑ Ask a tall learner to come to the board and touch the top of the board.
❑ Then write:
Lombe is tall. Therefore, she can touch the top of the board.
❑ Explain that in this type of expression of result, two sentences are used.
e.g. The bridge was swept away. Therefore, Mwila could not cross the river.
As a result,
For this reason,
Consequently,
Note that a comma is needed after the word used to express result.
❑ Explain the other ways of expressing result as follows:
Type 2: Using so to express result
The two ideas are joined into one sentence using the word so
A comma is needed before the word so.
e.g. Lombe is tall, so she can touch the top of the board
The bridge was swept away, so Mwila could not cross the river.
She learnt how to budget, so she saved enough money to buy a new bag.

11ENGLISH LANGUAGE TEACHING MODULE - TERM 2
Type 3: Using so…that to express result
In this type, the two ideas are joined by using so…that
Between so and that, there should be an adjective or an adverb.
e.g. Lombe is so tall that she can touch the top of the board
Annel was so careful that he did not contract Covid-19.
Type 4: Using such (a)…that to express result
In this type, such (a)…that is used to join two ideas into one sentence. The article a is used when
referring to a singular noun and is dropped when referring to a plural noun.
e.g. Lombe is such a tall girl that she can touch the top of the board
ACTIVITY 2: CONSTRUCTING SENTENCES TO EXPRESS RESULT

e.g.

DRAMA
INTRODUCTION
Overview: Drama is a type of storytelling performed on stage or screen, where actors portray
characters and emotions through dialogue and actions, often exploring conflicts and themes in
human life. Drama, as an art form, has been a powerful medium for storytelling for centuries, and
it continues to be relevant in various aspects of society. This topic will explore short plays, a form of
drama that tells interesting stories within a limited timeframe. It will examine their structure, key
elements, and how they reflect real-life issues. Short plays offer a condensed, yet impactful way to
engage with complex human emotions and situations, making them relevant in both entertainment
and life lessons.
General Competence(s):
❑ Communication:
 Effectively share one’s thoughts and emotions and understand others.
 Manage one’s emotions.
❑ Creativity and innovation:
 Organise information and ideas
 Justify one’s reasoning and procedures
 Ask thoughtful questions
Hook: Can someone make you laugh, cry, or rethink your own beliefs?
KEY TERMS /WORDS/VOCABULARY:
❑ Short Play: A concise form of drama typically lasting 15-30 minutes, focusing on brief yet
impactful storytelling.
❑ Character: The individuals in the play who perform actions, speak dialogue, and undergo
development throughout the story.
❑ Characterisation: The way in which an author develops characters, showing their personality
and growth throughout the play.
❑ Audience: The people who watch the performance, interpret the play, and respond to its
themes, emotions, and events.
❑ Narrator: (If present) A voice or character that provides background information or
commentary to help the audience understand the context or events.
❑ Scene: A specific section of the play, often occurring in a single location, where characters
interact and events unfold. Scenes typically change as the setting or time shifts.
❑ Act: A major division in the play, often consisting of several scenes. Acts help structure the
flow of the story and mark significant changes in the plot.
❑ Plot: The sequence of events in the play, including the conflict, climax, and resolution, that
drive the action and reveal the themes.
❑ Dialogue: The conversation between characters in a play, which drives the plot and reveals
character traits.
❑ Conflict: The central problem or challenge that drives the plot in a play, often between
characters or within oneself.

Siaciwena is too intelligent.
The party was too enjoyable.
These sentences do not make sense. For them to make sense we have to replace too with very.
He is very rich.
Siaciwena is very intelligent.
You should not use too when you mean very.
iii. Much means a large amount or to a large degree.
I feel much better.
I don’t earn much money
iv. Fairly and rather both mean ‘a little or moderately’. However, fairly suggests a more positive
attitude than rather.
Kisu is fairly tall.
I am fairly good at mathematics.
Vitumbiko is rather lazy.
v. Quite has two different uses. It can mean completely or a little less than.
My answer is quite different from yours. (completely).
Your composition is quite good. (a little less).
vi. Hardly/barely/scarcely are near negatives and mean almost not.
He hardly ever goes to church. (almost doesn’t)
We hardly had time to catch the train. (almost no time).
I was so tired I could scarcely keep my eyes open. (almost could not)
vii. Almost and nearly are used to mean very near but not quite there.
The match had almost finished when the captain scored.
viii. Just can have a similar meaning to barely, hardly and scarcely.
The party was just beginning when it started to rain.
He just managed to buy a car.
Just is often used with only.
We only just finished the exercise.

important tool for acquiring and sharing knowledge.

INTRODUCTION
Good communication is essential in both spoken and written language. One way we make

This topic will discuss how to check if what we are saying is correct or if the other person agrees

KEY TERMS/WORDS/VOCABULARY

seek agreement
❑ Auxiliary Verb (Helping Verb): A verb used with the main verb to form different tenses, voices,
or moods
❑ Positive Statement: A sentence that does not contain a negative word
❑ Negative Statement: A sentence that includes a negative word like not
❑ Positive Question Tag: A question tag that is positive and follows a negative statement
❑ Negative Question Tag: A question tag that is negative and follows a positive statement
❑ Short Answer: A brief response to a question tag, often using “Yes” or “No” followed by an
auxiliary verb
❑ Confirmation: The act of checking if information is true or correct using question tags
❑ Agreement: When someone responds to a statement with a question tag to show they agree
or disagree

❑ Questions tags are short questions added at the end of a statement to confirm or check

Rules

SUMMARY WRITING
TOPIC: SUMMARY
INTRODUCTION
Overview: A summary is a brief and concise piece of writing that presents only main points from a
given text (oral or written) without altering the meaning of the text. The ability to summarise a text
is an important skill in academic and social contexts in which there is endless need to make
information easier to communicate and understand. This topic, will discuss different types of
abbreviations and how to use them correctly in our everyday language.
General Competence(s):
❑ Communication:
 Express oneself using different media and symbols
 Communicate and relate effectively with others
❑ Creativity and innovation
 Organise information and ideas
Hook: Imagine you are texting a friend and want to send a message quickly. How would you shorten
your words to save time?
KEY TERMS/WORDS/VOCABULARY
❑ Abbreviation – A shortened form of a word or phrase.
❑ Clipping – A word that is shortened by removing one or more syllables
❑ Contraction – A shortened form of two words combined using an apostrophe
❑ Acronym – An abbreviation formed from the first letters of a phrase and pronounced as a
word
❑ Initialism – An abbreviation where each letter is pronounced separately
❑ Symbols – Characters used to represent words or concepts
❑ Ellipsis – The omission of letters or sounds in contractions
❑ Formal Writing – A style of writing where abbreviations are used less frequently.
❑ Informal Writing – A casual style of writing where abbreviations, symbols, and contractions
are common.
❑ Period (.) – A punctuation mark sometimes used in abbreviations.
SUB-TOPIC: ABBREVIATIONS
INTRODUCTION:
Abbreviations are shortened forms of words, phrases or sentences while maintaining the intended
meaning. Abbreviations help us communicate faster and more efficiently, whether we are texting,
writing, or even speaking. This topic will look at different ways of abbreviating words.
Specific competence: Apply abbreviations in writing

❑ Discuss the rules for using abbreviations
 Abbreviations should be used appropriately depending on the formality of the writing (e.g.,
abbreviations like ‘Dr.’ or ‘Mr.’ are acceptable in both formal and informal contexts, but

 Always use periods after some abbreviations (e.g., ‘Dr.’, ‘Mr.’), but not for others (e.g.,

ACTIVITY 2: DISCUSSING TYPES OF ABBREVIATIONS
❑ Display chart/use PowerPoint with abbreviations and discuss with the learners
Types of abbreviations
 Initialisms: composed of the first letter of each word (e.g., ‘ECZ’ for Examinations Council
of Zambia, ‘ATM’ for Automated Teller Machine, ‘UTH’ for University Teaching Hospital,
‘FB’ for Facebook).
 Acronyms: abbreviations formed from the first letter of a phrase but pronounced as a word
(e.g., ‘NASA’ for National Aeronautics and Space Administration, ‘ZESCO’ for Zambia
Electricity Supply Corporation).
 Clipping/Shortened words: some words are shortened (e.g., ‘Dr.’ for Doctor, ‘Inc.’ for
Incorporated, ‘Exam’ for examination, ‘Phone’ for Telephone, ‘Ad’ for Advertisement …).
 Dates and Time: abbreviations related to dates and time (e.g., ‘Mon.’ for Monday, ‘AM’ for
ante Meridiem, ‘PM’ post Meridiem).
 Contraction: a combination of two words, where some letters are omitted and replaced by
an apostrophe (‘) (e.g., can’t - cannot, I’m – I am, he’s – he is …)
 Symbols: are used to replace words or numbers in writing especially in informal
communication (e.g., & - And, @ - At, # - Number/Hashtag, % - Percent, $ - Dollar, K -
Kwacha …)
