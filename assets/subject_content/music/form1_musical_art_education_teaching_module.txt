The Competence-Based Curriculum (CBC) is designed to equip learners with competences
necessary to navigate the complexities of the modern world. This Musical Arts Education
(MAE) teaching module is tailored to support teachers in implementing the CBC, focusing on
the attainment of specific competences of specified sub-topics as indicated in the syllabus, but
aligned to the general competences like creativity and innovation, critical thinking, problem-
solving, and effective communication.
Each specific competence is supported by carefully designed learning activities that cater for
diverse learning styles and abilities. These learning activities further give guidance on how they
should be effectively administered to the learners to meet the expected standards that speak to the
specific competences. Furthermore, this module encourages teachers to assess learner progress
using a range of strategies.
By using this MAE teaching module, teachers will be able to support learners to acquire
competences outlined in the syllabus. This will enable the development of practical skills,
enhancement of talents, creativity, self-expression and sense of aesthetics among learners.

TOPIC 1.9 TONE COLOUR (TIMBRE)
Introduction
Tone color, also known as timbre, refers to the distinct quality or character of a sound that allows
us to differentiate between different voices or instruments, even if they are producing the same
pitch and loudness. It’s what makes a violin sound different from a flute, even if they’re playing
the same note.
It is an important element of music that helps musicians express emotion and style, create
contrast and texture in music, identify and blend different instruments or voices in an ensemble.
A number of adjectives can be used to describe tone colour. Some of these include; bright, dark,
warm, nasal, smooth, harsh, mellow, piercing …
General Competences
By the end of the topic, learners will acquire the following competences:
Analytical Thinking: Ability to systematically examine, interpret and evaluate Pitch for purpose
of applying them in addressing social-economical needs
Collaboration: Ability to work effectively with others in various musical setting such as
ensembles or bands and other music projects to achieve goals in Musical Arts Education.
Communication: Ability to express and convey musical ideas appropriately, clearly and
effectively. (verbal and non-verbal)
Creativity and innovation: Ability to experiment new approaches and generate musical ideas
that exhibit originality and take creative risks in the composition, performance and interpretation
of music
Critical thinking: Ability to analyse, investigate and evaluate pitch, musical performances and
practices with a discerning and reflective mindset to address social-economical needs.
Terminology
Articulation: How individual notes are played or sung, affecting their clarity, style and
expression.
Breath control: A singer’s or wind player’s ability to manage their breathing to produce
steady, controlled and expressive sound.
Diaphragmatic breathing: (Also known as belly or deep breathing) is a breathing
technique where you use your diaphragm to take in air efficiently.
Ensemble: A group of musicians performing together as a unit.
Indigenous instruments: traditional instruments specific to a culture or religion.
Register: Specific range of the voice that has a distinct tone colour, vocal quality and
physiological function.
Resonance: The way sound vibrations are amplified and enriched as they pass through
the vocal tract and body.

SUB-TOPIC 1.9.1 VOCAL TIMBRES
Introduction
Vocal timbres refer to the unique qualities of a singer’s voice that distinguish it from others. Just
as with instrumental timbres, vocal timbre is what makes one singer’s voice sound different from
another, even if they are singing the same note. It is shaped by various factors such as the shape
of the vocal cords, the resonance of the vocal tract, and the technique used in singing.

SUB-TOPIC: 1.11.2 HANDLING AND PLAYING MUSICAL INSTRUMENTS
introduction
Playing a musical instrument is not just an artistic pursuit; it’s a skill that requires dedication,
practice, and, crucially, the proper care and maintenance of an instrument.
A musical instrument is not merely an object; it is an extension of a musical identity and a
conduit for artistic expression. Whether one plays a guitar, piano, violin, or any other instrument,
keeping it in excellent condition is paramount for several reasons:
1. Sound Quality
Proper maintenance ensures that an instrument maintains its beautiful tone. It prevents issues
like buzzing strings, out-of-tune keys, or muted sound, allowing one to create rich and melodious
tones desired.
2. Longevity
Regular care and maintenance can significantly extend the lifespan of an instrument. Instruments
are often a significant investment, and preserving their condition safeguards financial commitment.
3. Playability
A well-maintained instrument is more comfortable and enjoyable to play. It responds better to
touch and facilitates smoother practice sessions and performances.
4. Cost Savings
Neglecting maintenance can lead to costly repairs or even the need to replace your instrument
entirely. Proper care is often more cost-effective in the long run.
Maintenance Tips for Common Instruments
Each type of musical instrument has its unique set of requirements for care and maintenance.
Here, we’ll delve into the specifics of maintaining some of the most popular instruments:
1. String Instruments (Violin, Guitar, …)
a. Cleaning: After each practice session, gently wipe down the strings and body of the
instrument with a soft, clean cloth to remove rosin, dirt, and oils.
b. String Replacement: Change strings regularly, as they can lose their tonal quality and
become prone to breakage.
c. Humidity Control: Storing a string instrument in a humidity-controlled environment to
prevent warping, cracking, and other damage that can occur due to changes in moisture
levels.
2. Piano
a. Tuning: Regularly schedule piano tuning with a professional to maintain the correct
pitch.
b. Cleaning: Dust the keys and clean the exterior of a piano with a soft, slightly damp
cloth. Avoid using chemicals or excessive moisture that could damage the finish.

c. Regulation: Have a technician check the regulation of a piano to ensure even key

3. Wind Instruments (Flute, Saxophone, …
a. Swabbing: After playing, clean the moisture from the inside of the instrument using a
swab.
b. Valve and Slide Maintenance: For brass instruments, regularly lubricate valves and
slides to prevent sticking or sluggish movement.
c. Pad Replacement: Check and replace pads as needed for woodwind instruments to

4. Digital Instruments
In today’s digital age, electronic instruments like keyboards and synthesizers also require
specific care and maintenance:
a. Dust and Dirt: Keep the instrument covered when not in use to prevent dust buildup. Use
a soft, damp cloth to clean the keys and surfaces, avoiding excessive moisture.
b. Power Protection: Invest in surge protectors to safeguard against electrical surges that
can damage electronic components.
c. Software Updates: Stay current with software updates and ensure your instrument’s
firmware is up to date for optimal performance.
5. Drums
Drums are a diverse family of percussion instruments, and they require specific care and
maintenance to ensure they sound their best and remain in good condition.
a. Cleaning: After each drumming session, use a soft, clean cloth or drum-specific cleaning
products to wipe down drumheads and shells. This helps remove sweat, dust, and stick
marks, which can affect the sound quality.
b. Tuning: Regularly check and adjust the tension of a drumheads to maintain the desired
pitch and tone. One can use a drum key to make these adjustments.
c. Cymbal Care: Keep cymbals clean by using a cymbal cleaner or a mixture of water
and mild soap. Avoid abrasive materials that can scratch the cymbal surface. Regularly
inspect cymbals for cracks or key holing, and replace any damaged ones promptly.
d. Hardware maintenance: Inspect the drum hardware, including tension rods, lugs, and
screws, for any loose or damaged parts. Tighten or replace them as needed to prevent
issues during playing.

only encompass the production of various sounds. It has become an increasingly important tool in
music education in schools. By incorporating music technology into music lessons, learners gain
access to a wide range of resources and tools that can help them learn and express themselves
through music.
It enables artists, producers, and musicians to create, record, and share music in new and
innovative ways.

the use of digital audio workstations (DAWs) and music production software. These tools allow

Types of Audio Hardware:
1. Audio Input Devices: these are devices that function as audio signal receivers and
are often inserted on the input port of the appropriate device. Mixer (Signal Processor):
Mixers come in different sizes and the size of a mixer is normally dependent on the
number of channels it has. The smallest size normally has 2 channels, while medium
sized range from 12 to 32 channels. Large format mixers can range from 48 channels to
even 128 channels.
2. Audio Output devices: these are devices whose function is to send audio signals
through the output port.
3. Audio Signal Processors and Effectors: these are devices that are used to modify
audio signals. They often have input and output ports on the front or back panel of the
device itself.
4. Audio Cables: these are accessories used to connect input and output devices in an
audio signal chain or sound system.

 SIGNAL PATH: This is the route a sound takes from the source to the output.
This usually goes from the source of sound (instrument or voice) to the microphone,
then to the audio interface (sound card) into the software (DAW) through the
processor then it is mixed and mastered within the software finally returned to
the interface before the monitors(speakers) for playback.
