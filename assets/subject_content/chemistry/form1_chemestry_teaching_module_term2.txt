Safety in the learning environment is a requirement in learning Chemistry, as learners are exposed to

identification, personal protective equipment, emergency response plans, chemical handling, storage,
and disposal, and learner responsibilities. Prioritising safety minimises risks, prevents accidents, and
ensures a positive learning experience.

Topic 3: Elements, Compounds and Mixtures
Introduction
In nature, substances exist in different forms. Some exist in their native form (uncombined form),
while others are in combined forms. This topic will explore different types of substances in nature.
Substances can be classified into elements, compounds and mixtures. The topic will look at these
three classes of substances in terms of what they are, their properties and differences, and how they
are represented diagrammatically.

Sub-Topic 1: Elements
Introduction
Everything in our environment, from the air we breathe in, the food we eat to the gadgets we use on
a daily basis, are made up of elements. Elements are actually so essential to our everyday existence
that we often take them for granted. This sub topic will look at what elements are, their classification
and diagrammatic representation.
Specific Competence:
 Demonstrate understanding of elements in everyday life
Key Terms:
 Element: A pure substance that has only one kind of atoms and cannot be chemically broken
down into simpler substances
 Metallic elements: A group of elements that ionise by losing electrons and exhibit
characteristic properties such as shiny, electrical conductivity and high melting and boiling
points
 Non-metallic elements: A group of elements that ionise by gaining electrons and exhibit
characteristic properties such as dull, brittle, and poor electrical conductivity
Learning Activities

Introduction
Most elements do not exist in their native (un combined) form. Such elements occur in form of
compounds. Compounds are pure substances formed when two or more different elements chemically
combine in a fixed ratio. Compounds have several uses and applications in our daily life. Some of the
uses and applications of compounds include development of new materials such as medicines,
fertilizers and plastics. This sub topic will explore compounds in everyday life and their diagrammatic
representation. Furthermore, the sub topic will analyse the different characteristics of compounds.

compound

Sub-Topic 5: Criteria of Purity
Introduction
This subtopic teaches learners about the concept of purity, which is determined by consistent
properties like melting point, boiling point, and density. It emphasizes its importance in fields like
science, medicine, and industry. Learners will learn how to test for purity and its impact on properties
and applications in pharmaceuticals, food science, and environmental science. Through discussions,
demonstrations, and hands-on experiments, learners will develop practical skills for assessing
substance purity.
Specific Competence:
 Determine the purity of a substance
Key Terms:
 Boiling Point: The temperature at which vapour pressure of the liquid equals the atmospheric
pressure
 Chromatography: A separation technique used to separate a mixture of solutes using a solvent
and a separating medium
 Chromatogram: This is a visual or graphical representation of the separation of components
in a mixture, produced by chromatography
 Density: The mass of a substance per unit volume
 Impurities: Uunwanted substances or contaminants mixed with another substance
 Melting Point: The temperature at which a solid change into a liquid
 Mobile phase: A liquid or gas phase that moves through the stationary phase
 Purity: The extent to which a substance is free from contamination
 Refractive Index: A dimensionless number that describes how light propagates through a
medium
 Stationary phase: It is a solid or liquid phase that remains fixed
Learning Activities
The learning activities have been developed to help learners determine substance purity. Through
experiments and analysis, learners will learn techniques to determine purity, such as measuring
melting and boiling points, and chromatography, to understand the importance of purity in science
and everyday life. The two learning activities engages learners in hands-on activities to understand
purity determination methods.

Sub-Topic 6: Purification and Separation Techniques
Introduction:
Purification and separation techniques are important in Chemistry for isolating components from
mixtures to obtain pure substances. Separation of mixtures can be done using various techniques.
This requires understanding the principles behind each separation technique, the nature of the mixture
to be separated and the component(s) of interest in the mixture.
Specific Competence:
 Separate mixtures into their desired components
Key Terms:
 Centrifugation: A separation technique used to separate suspended particles in a liquid by
applying a rapid spinning motion to a mixture
 Chromatography: A separation technique used to separate a mixture of solutes using a solvent
and a separating medium
 Crystallisation: The process of obtaining crystals from a homogeneous solution
 Decantation: The process of separating liquid from solid residues by pouring off the liquid
without disturbing the sediment

 Distillation: A separation technique that involves heating a liquid to form vapour and then
condensing the vapour back into a liquid

filter medium that allows the fluid to pass through while retaining the solid particles
