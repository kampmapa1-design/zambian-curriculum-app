ECZ Religious Education Paper 1 (2046/1), 2016, Question 1: (a) Relate the story of the birth of Jesus in Bethlehem (Luke 2:1-7). (b) Name two Jewish infant ceremonies and when each took place. (c) Give Zambian and modern reasons for valuing/celebrating a child's birth. (d) Compare Christian and Muslim teaching on God's direction of human life. Model answer: Luke 2:1-7 tells how a Roman census under Caesar Augustus (while Quirinius governed Syria) required everyone to register in their home town; Joseph travelled from Nazareth to Bethlehem (David's town) with Mary, who was expecting a child, because he belonged to David's line. While there, Mary gave birth to her first son, wrapped him in cloth and laid him in a manger, since there was no room at the inn. Jewish parents marked a newborn son's life with ceremonies including circumcision (on the eighth day), dedication (about a month old) and the mother's purification (forty days after birth). In Zambia, birth customs and gifts express appreciation of new life, give the child identity, affirm the continuity of life, and mark the child's future value to the community; modern Zambian society shows the same appreciation by naming the child, caring for and protecting the baby, giving gifts, and holding celebrations. Christian and Muslim teaching agree that God directs every person's life, that God has a purpose for each life, and that everything that happens reflects God's will.

ECZ Religious Education Paper 1 (2046/1), 2016, Question 2: (a) Describe how God instructed Abraham to keep the covenant through circumcision (Genesis 17:10-14). (b) Explain the importance of circumcision to the Jews. (c) Give Christian advice on the meaning of water baptism, and modern reasons for male circumcision. (d) Compare Christian and Islamic admission/initiation practices. Model answer: In Genesis 17:10-14, God instructs Abraham that every male in his household -- including slaves -- must be circumcised at eight days old as a permanent, physical sign of the covenant between Abraham's descendants and God; anyone not circumcised would be considered to have broken the covenant. Circumcision mattered to the Jews as an outward sign of their covenant relationship with God, a direct command from God, a mark of belonging to God's people, and a condition of Jewish national identity. To a new believer questioning the need for water baptism, Christian teaching explains that baptism is an outward sign of an inward conversion, initiates a believer into the church, identifies the believer with Christ's death, burial and resurrection, and publicly expresses repentance and faith already present in the heart; today, male circumcision is also valued for reducing the risk of transmitting HIV and other sexually transmitted infections, and cervical cancer in female partners. Christianity and Islam differ in admission practice: Christian initiation typically involves instruction from a church leader and a ceremony (such as immersion in water), whereas Islam has no equivalent formal childhood initiation ceremony into adult religious life.

ECZ Religious Education Paper 1 (2046/1), 2016, Question 3: (a) Narrate Jesus healing the paralytic man (Luke 5:17-26). (b) State why Jesus healed the sick. (c) Give Christian advice on caring for a chronically sick spouse, and society's attitude to the chronically sick today. (d) Compare Christian and Zambian traditional attitudes to the sick. Model answer: In Luke 5:17-26, friends of a paralysed man, unable to reach Jesus through the crowd, lower him through an opening in the roof; seeing their faith, Jesus first forgives the man's sins (provoking accusations of blasphemy from the Pharisees and teachers of the law present) and then heals him, telling him to pick up his mat and go home -- which he does, praising God, to the amazement of everyone present. Jesus healed the sick out of compassion and love, to restore both physical and spiritual life, and in response to people's faith. Christian teaching calls for a spouse facing chronic illness to be treated with sympathy, care, love, compassion and kindness; more broadly, chronically sick people in society today should be accepted, loved, cared for, helped, and never stigmatised or discriminated against. Christianity and Zambian tradition both emphasise caring for the sick, seeking a cure, and showing sympathy towards them.

(c) Give Christian advice on driving out evil spirits, and identify types of spirits recognised in modern Zambian society. (d) Contrast Christian and Zambian Traditional Religion's treatment of spirit possession. Model answer: In Luke 9:37-43, a man begs Jesus to help his only son, repeatedly seized and thrown into fits by an evil spirit that Jesus's disciples had been unable to cast out; Jesus rebukes the unbelief around him, commands the spirit, heals the boy and returns him to his father, to the amazement of the crowd at God's power. Christianity relies on prayer and scripture to cast out spirits and faith in Jesus for deliverance, whereas Zambian Traditional Religion typically uses drumming, charms and lotions, and relies on charms and amulets for protection.

(c) Give Christian advice on entering God's kingdom, and on how people today show they are unprepared for judgement. (d) Contrast Christian and Hindu beliefs about personal judgement. Model answer: In Luke 12:35-40, Jesus tells of servants who stay dressed and ready with their lamps lit, awaiting their master's return from a wedding feast so they can open the door for him at once, whenever he comes -- warning that the Son of Man will likewise come at an hour no one expects, like a thief in the night, so his followers must always be ready. From this parable, Christians learn to remain spiritually alert at all times, that Christ's coming will be sudden and unannounced, and that those found ready will be rewarded. Entering the kingdom of God requires more than religious attendance -- Christian advice points to repentance, righteous living, obedience to God, and faith in Christ; conversely, people show they are unprepared for judgement through lack of faith, unrepentant hearts, disobedience, unbelief, and excessive love of worldly things. Christianity teaches judgement based on one's response to Christ, on a final day of judgement, with belief in resurrection; Hinduism instead teaches judgement based on one's deeds (karma), occurring soon after death, with belief in reincarnation.

(c) Give Christian encouragement for unanswered prayer, and modern views on how God answers prayer. (d) Contrast Christian and Zambian Traditional Religion's approach to prayer. Model answer: In Luke 11:5-13, a man persistently asks a friend for bread at midnight to feed an unexpected guest; though reluctant at first, the friend eventually gets up and gives him what he needs because of his persistence -- illustrating Jesus's teaching to 'ask, seek, knock', since even flawed human parents give their children good gifts, so God will surely give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him. This parable teaches Christians the importance and value of persistence and faith in prayer. To someone discouraged by seemingly unanswered prayer, Christian encouragement is to keep praying, keep trusting and depending on God, and to remember that God answers in his own time and no prayer is offered in vain; in modern society, people understand unanswered prayer variously as 'no' (because the request was not God's will), 'yes' (seen in God's help through difficult situations), or 'wait' (because the right time has not yet come). Christianity holds that anyone may pray, at any time, without sacrifice; Zambian Traditional Religion typically reserves prayer for special occasions (such as funerals or drought), channelled through an appointed elder or priest, often with accompanying sacrifice.

Model answer: In Luke 4:16-30, Jesus reads from Isaiah in the Nazareth synagogue and declares the prophecy fulfilled in himself; though the congregation is initially impressed, they turn furious when he suggests -- citing Elijah and Elisha's ministry to non-Israelites -- that a prophet is not accepted in his own hometown, and they drive him to a cliff intending to throw him off, but he walks safely through the crowd. Jesus responded to this hostility calmly and without retaliation, exposing his opponents' unbelief while simply walking away untouched. Facing criticism for taking an unpopular stand (such as refusing to join a protest), Christian advice holds that suffering for doing right, not wrong, brings no shame, and that aggressive reactions cause disunity and fail to reflect Christian values of love, tolerance and forgiveness; in practice, people today react to criticism with anything from resentment and blame-shifting to genuine acceptance and self-improvement. Christian and Gandhian responses to opposition are similar in that both reject revenge, both respond with love, forgiveness and tolerance, and both practise non-violence even under persecution.

This shows Christians that Jesus is the Son of God with power over death itself, the resurrection and the life. Christianity and Hinduism both affirm some form of life after death and both hold that physical death separates the soul from the body.

ECZ Religious Education Paper 1 (2046/1), 2016, Question 10: (a) Tell the Parable of the Workers in the Vineyard. (b) What different attitudes towards work do people hold? (c) Give Christian teaching on fair wages, and how to show respect for manual workers today. (d) Compare Christian and Zambian traditional attitudes to work. Model answer: In the parable, a landowner hires workers at different times throughout the day, but pays every worker the same wage at day's end; when those hired first complain, he replies that he has kept his agreement with them and is free to be as generous as he wishes with his own money -- illustrating that 'the last will be first, and the first will be last.' People hold varied attitudes to work: some see it purely as a means to meet basic needs, others genuinely enjoy it, others resent (especially manual) labour, and some are simply lazy. Christian teaching on wages holds that workers must be paid promptly and that wages must be just and fair, not withheld or minimised regardless of a worker's training; respect for manual workers today can be shown by accepting them as they are, appreciating their skills, and recognising the valuable role they play in society. Christianity and Zambian tradition agree that everyone should work, that laziness should be discouraged, and that one who will not work should not expect to eat.

Christianity and Zambian tradition agree in valuing respect for parents and loyalty among family members.
