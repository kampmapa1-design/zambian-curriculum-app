1.11 CRAFTS IN 2D
Introduction
The topic of “Crafts in 2D” focuses on the exploration and creation of two-dimensional art
forms, emphasizing the development of essential skills such as analytical thinking, creativity,
and collaboration. Through engaging in various crafting techniques, learners will learn to
express their ideas visually while enhancing their understanding of artistic concepts. This topic
encourages learners to experiment with different materials and methods, fostering innovation and
critical thinking in their artistic endeavours. This topic will cover Picture making and Weaving
General Competences:
Analytical Thinking, Communication, Creativity and Innovation, Collaboration, Critical
Thinking, Problem Solving
Key Terms/Words
 Collage: A technique that combines various materials (like paper and fabric) onto a
surface to create a cohesive artwork, displaying different textures and colours.
 Montage: An artistic method that merges multiple images or scenes into one
composition, often to tell a story or convey a theme through juxtaposition.
 Mosaic: An art form involving the assembly of small coloured pieces (glass, stone,
or tiles) to create intricate images or patterns.
 Frieze: A decorative horizontal band in architecture, featuring reliefs or paintings,
or an ongoing pictorial narrative in art.
 Warp: The lengthwise threads held in tension on a loom during weaving, providing
the framework for the fabric.
 Weft: The threads woven across the warp to create fabric, typically interlaced at
right angles to the warp.
 Loom: A device used for weaving fabric, securing the warp threads and facilitating
the interweaving of the weft.

1.13 SCULPTURE
Introduction
In this topic, the focus is on the Introduction to Sculpture. Sculpture is the art of making solid
figures or objects with representations of length, breadth, height or width and depth. It is a three
dimensional art form also referred to as art “in the round” as one can see all sides of the product.
These solid structures are made free standing and sometimes fixed to a firm ground. The solid
figure or object is also known as a sculpture and the composer of the art pieces is referred to as
a Sculptor. The size of sculptures ranges from tiny figurines of carved lizards or modelled frogs
to life size statues of human and wild life representations.
General Competences:
 Analytical Thinking
 Communication
 Critical Thinking
Key Terms/Words
 Sculpture: a three-dimensional art form created by shaping or combining materials
(stone, metal, clay, or wood…)
 Freestanding sculptures: are three-dimensional works that are not attached to a
background or surface. They can be viewed from all sides.
 Relief sculpture: a type of sculpture in which the figures are carved into a background
surface, creating a raised effect. Reliefs can be classified as low (bas-relief) or high
(haut-relief) depending on the depth of the projection.
 Marquette: a small-scale model or prototype of a sculpture, used by artists to plan
and visualize the final piece.
 Additive sculpture: created by adding material to build up a shape or form. Common
Materials (clay, wax …)
 Subtractive sculpture: involves removing material from a solid block (stone or
Wood…) to create the desired shape. This technique often uses tools to carve and
refine the piece.
 Casting: a process in which a liquid material (metal, plaster, or resin…) is poured
into a mould to create a sculpture. Once it hardens, the mould is removed, revealing
the final form.
 Construction in sculpture: assembling different materials (wood, metal, Plastic…)
to create a three-dimensional artwork.
 Assemblage: a form of sculpture that combines various found objects and materials
into a cohesive artwork. This technique emphasizes the creative use of everyday
items.

SUBTOPIC: 1.13.3 CARVING
Introduction
In this subtopic, learners shall be exposed to carving in different forms and different materials.
To carve means to make a form by cutting or chipping away parts of a given material. It is a
sculptural technique that involves removing material from a solid block to create a desired shape
or design. Carving should be carried according to the medium. Wood carving for instance, should
be done across the grains and when it comes to putting the final touches, it should be treated to
reveal the natural pattern formed by the grains.

1.14 ENTREPRENEURSHIP
Introduction
Entrepreneurship is a dynamic and innovative field that enables individuals to turn their passions
into successful ventures. This topic explores the intersection of art and business, where creativity
meets commerce. Learners will discover how to define entrepreneurship, leverage their artistic
talents as a business, and appreciate the value of art in the market.
By the end of this topic, learners are expected to effectively utilize art as a business, showcasing
their unique perspective and skills to create innovative and profitable ventures.
General Competences: Analytical Thinking, Communication, Creativity and Innovation,
Collaboration, Critical Thinking, Environmental Sustainability, Financial Literacy, Problem
Solving and Entrepreneurship
Key Terms /Words/Vocabulary:
 Innovation: Creating new ideas, products, or services that add value.
 Business: An organization or enterprise that provides goods or services to customers.
 Risk: The possibility of loss or failure in a business venture.
 Start-up: A new business or company in its early stages of development.
 Market: A place or platform where goods or services are bought and sold.
 Product: A tangible or intangible item offered to customers.
 Service: An intangible offering that provides value to customers.
 Strategy: A plan of action designed to achieve business goals.
 Investment: The act of putting money or resources into a business to generate
returns.
 Profit: The financial gain earned by a business after deducting expenses.

LESSON EVALUATION:
Having taught the lesson, 65 out of the 75 learners present, managed to achieve the expected
lesson standards and competences. 10 out of the 75 learners did not achieve the expected lesson
competences and standards, this was due to their inability to achieve first stages of sketching and
drawing the objects in good time. The learners were then given remedial time during prep time
and given carry-home work.
The teacher was able to ensure collaboration was upheld throughout the lesson and gave individual
learner attention at sketching and drawing stages.
Overall, the lesson was successfully attained at about 86% rate.
