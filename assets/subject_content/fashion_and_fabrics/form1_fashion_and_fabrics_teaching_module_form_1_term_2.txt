Fashion and fabrics are an important part of learners’ daily lives. They are not just about looking
good; they are about learners expressing themselves and being creative. Learning about fashion and
fabrics will help learners understand how clothes are made, how different materials work, and how
to create and design their own styles. This module is all about giving them the knowledge and skills
to explore the world of the fashion industry.
This module introduces learners to essential concepts in garment construction and textile work,
covering Needlework Tools and Equipment, Fibres and Fabrics, Clothing Design, and Construction
Processes. Learners will build foundational knowledge, enhance creativity, and apply skills in
practical contexts such as home use, cultural expression, or entrepreneurship. The module emphasizes
hands-on learning, critical thinking, and appreciation for both modern and traditional textile
approaches.
Needlework Tools and Equipment familiarise learners with the essential tools for sewing, including
needles, scissors, and sewing machines. Emphasis is placed on tool usage, safety, maintenance, and
fostering good habits to ensure accuracy and efficiency in garment -making.
Fibres and Fabrics covers the properties, origins, and classifications of materials, helping learners
choose fabrics suited for different projects. The topic also introduces sustainable practices,
encouraging learners to make environmentally conscious decisions when selecting materials based
on functionality, durability, and aesthetics.
Clothing Design encourages creativity and problem-solving, focusing on design principles such as
colour, shape, and proportion. learners learn to sketch designs, considering body types, functionality,
and cultural influences, while exploring how clothing reflects personal style and identity.
Construction Processes takes students through the steps of garment creation, from measurements
and pattern drafting to stitching and finishing. This topic teaches precision, patience, and planning,
allowing learners to develop technical skills that can lead to careers in tailoring, fashion design, or
personal garment-making.

1.5.1 Sub-topic: Needlework tools
INTRODUCTION
Needlework has played a significant role throughout human history, evolving from a practical
necessity into a popular creative hobby. From sewing garments and mending fabrics to designing
detailed embroidery and quilts, the art of needlework relies heavily on the use of proper tools. These
tools, including needles, tape measures and scissors, are essential for creating clean, durable, and
professional work. Understanding how each tool functions allow beginners to build confidence and
improve the quality of their work.
To become skilled in needlework, it is important to master the use, choice, care, and storage of various
tools. High-quality, durable tools are recommended over cheaper alternatives, as they provide better
performance and longevity. Proper maintenance and careful handling are crucial to ensuring the tools
remain in good condition. This foundational knowledge helps both beginners and experienced crafters
achieve precision and efficiency in their sewing and embroidery projects.

Importance of Colour fastness and Fabric Preparation Before Dyeing
1. Colour fastness: Why It Matters
Colour fastness refers to the ability of fabric to retain its colour without fading, bleeding, or
transferring when exposed to washing, sunlight, sweat, or rubbing. It is an essential factor in fabric
dyeing for several reasons:
• Prevents Fading: Ensures the fabric maintains its original vibrancy over time.
• Avoids Bleeding: Prevents colours from running or mixing with other garments during
washing.
• Enhances Durability: Ensures the dye bonds well with the fibres, making the colour long-
lasting.
• Improves Consumer Satisfaction: Poor colour fastness can lead to dissatisfaction with
garments that fade quickly.
To improve colour fastness, fixatives like salt, vinegar, or commercial mordant (for natural dyes)
are often used to set the dye.

The seven (7) elements of design are the basic building blocks used to create visual
compositions. A quick summary of each:
• Line – A mark with greater length than width. Lines can be straight, curved, thick, thin, or

45

• Shape – A closed line that creates a flat, two -dimensional area. Shapes can be geometric (like

• Colour – The hue, saturation, and brightness of an object. Colour can set a mood, attract

(sculptures) or implied (through shading in a drawing).
• Value – The lightness or darkness of a colour or tone. Value helps create contrast, depth, and
emphasis in a design.

The garment construction process involves the steps required to transform raw materials (fabrics,

Key Terms:
Key words Meanings
Fabric A piece of cloth or textile.
Cutting The process of cutting fabric into pieces based on the pattern design.
Sewing Joining fabric pieces using stitches, either by hand or machine.
Seam The line where two pieces of fabric are sewn together.
Fitting The process of ensuring the garment fits correctly on the body.
Hemming The finishing of the garment's edge to prevent fraying, usually done by

Trim Decorative or functional additions like zippers, buttons, lace, or appliqué.
Pressing The act of smoothing fabric with an iron to remove wrinkles and shape the
garment.
Tacking Temporary stitches used to hold fabric pieces in place before the final
stitching.

Pressing Technique
• Lift and lower the iron rather than sliding it to avoid stretching the fabric.
• Apply gentle pressure to avoid damage.
• Use steam or a damp cloth for stubborn wrinkles.
Ironing Technique
• Move the iron in smooth, straight motions along the grain of the fabric.
• Start with low heat and increase if necessary.
• Avoid excessive pressure to prevent shine or fabric distortion.
Finishing
• Allow the fabric to cool before handling to set the pressed areas.
• Store the ironed fabric properly to maintain smoothness.
