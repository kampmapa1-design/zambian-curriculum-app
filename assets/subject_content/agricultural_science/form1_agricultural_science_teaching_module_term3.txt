The Agricultural Science Form 1 Teaching Module has been developed to guide teachers on how to
facilitate Competence-based lessons at this level. The module aims to provide quality education that
is aligned with the Competence-Based Curriculum and 21st Century Skills, that is developing
knowledge, skills, values and positive attitudes that are aimed at enabling Learners to live and grow
into productive and useful members of their communities and the Zambian society.
Agricultural science will be taught using learner centred activities which will help learners to acquire
desirable lifelong skills, values and positive attitudes needed for their personal and national
development. Agricultural Science, as an applied science, will be taught in a practical manner, in
which learners will be involved in carrying out agricultural activities which will include growing
crops, rearing poultry, keeping livestock, apiculture and aquaculture.
Therefore, Agricultural Science activities will be done both in the laboratory where experiments will
be conducted and, in the field where field trials/observations will be done.
The assessment in the teaching module is part of the School-Based Assessment. It will be
emphasised by using various methods of testing according to sub-topics as part of the normal
teaching and learning process and therefore, the assessment opportunities will also occur
during this normal process.
The assessment to measure each specific competence will occur in three forms:
 Observation – watching learners working (good for assessing skills and values)
 Conversation – asking questions and talking to learners (good for assessing knowledge and
understanding)
 Product – appraising the learners’ work (writing reports, translation, calculations, presentations,
maps, diagrams, models etc.) in this context the product is seen as something physical and
permanent that the teacher can keep and look at.

The purpose of the module is to illustrate teaching and learning activities that:
i. are consistent with teaching and learning in a competence-based curriculum,
ii. show processes that allow achievement of 21 st Century Skills such as creativity, collaboration,
communication and critical thinking,
iii. illustrate what activity-based and learner-centred teaching and learning could look like.

HOW TO USE THE MODULE
This module provides suggested learning activities and teaching strategies that enhance learners’
scientific competences. The suggested activities are designed to offer learners hands-on experiences
through manipulation of real objects, interaction with nature and learning through practice, bearing
in mind that the desired competences and 21st century skills will NOT be achieved by the topics
‘covered’ but by the manner in which teaching and learning activities are carried out.
Activities in the Module are meant to be incorporated in the teacher’s lesson plan. The activities are
NOT meant to replace the teacher’s lesson plan.
The topics and activities in the Module are as specified in the 2024 Agricultural Science Ordinary
Level Syllabus. There might be terms used in the Module that are not the same as those in the syllabus,
but this is in the interest of using language that might be more appropriate for the learner. Teachers
are encouraged to be mindful as well about what language is within the grasp of their learners and
not use terms simply because they appear in the syllabus.
Teachers are encouraged to start topics with a hook that provokes their learners’ curiosity and
motivation. A Hook is an opening statement at the start of the topic that seeks to stimulate learners’
curiosity and motivate them to learn. A hook can be a provocative question, a scenario or something
that puts learners into a thinking mood. Let the designed hook incline towards problem-solving
scenarios.

TOPIC 6.0: LIVESTOCK PRODUCTION
Introduction:
Livestock production is a practice of raising and maintaining domesticated animals primarily for the
purpose of producing meat, milk and eggs. By so doing, livestock production contributes significantly
to food security, income generation and rural development.
By allowing learners to visit farms, learners will identify the livestock species farmers keep,
appreciate the value of livestock production to the household, community, and nation, and experience
some of the challenges farmers face in raising livestock. They will not only identify challenges but
be part of the carder of skilled individuals that will devise practical ways of addressing the problem.
Both experiential and demonstration methods will be used in teaching this topic.
General Competence(s):
 Analytical thinking,
 Communication,
 Creativity and innovation,
 Collaboration
 Critical Thinking,
 Environmental sustainability and
 Problem solving.
Key Concepts/Processes/Terms
Ration - Is the daily measured amount of feed given to an animal to meet both its maintenance
and production requirements.
Digestibility – The proportion of the feed that is digested absorbed and utilised by the animal.
Ration formulation - The process of combining feed ingredients to make a diet that meets an
animal's specific nutrient requirements.
Concentrates - These feeds of low moisture, low fibre but high in digestible nutrients.
Feed additives - Substances, microorganisms, or preparations added to animal feed or water
to enhance animal health, performance, or the quality of the feed itself or the food products derived
from animals.

Tip:
Learners may use the following:
Pearson’s Square Method
i. Draw a Pearson’s Square.
ii. Draw diagonal lines inside the square.
iii. On the two left corners (top and bottom) of the square, insert the percentage nutrient
composition of the two different ingredients you are mixing.
iv. Write the percentage composition of the value of the targeted amount of nutrient (DCP%) in
the middle of the square.
v. Subtract diagonally across the square the small number from the large one, regardless of the
sign, and
vi. Write the resultant values on the right-side corners. Resulting values give the proportions of
ingredients to be added.
vii. Express the proportions of the ingredients out of 100kg.
