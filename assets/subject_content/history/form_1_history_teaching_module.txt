The History Form 1 Teaching Module has been prepared by the Ministry of Education under
the Directorate of Curriculum Development to kick start the implementation of the 2024
Competence Based Curriculum.
The Module covers some of the topics offered at form 1 level, particularly in term 2. The
module has been designed in alignment to the 2024 Competence Based Curriculum. The
material covered is in full response to the demands and dictates of the new Curriculum.
The new curriculum is Competent Based and focuses on applied skills and knowledge that
enable learners to successfully perform in professional, educational and other life contents.
The Module is therefore, aimed at producing a learner that will be globally accepted based
on their vast interpretation of the past, present and the future.
The suggested learner activities are designed to offer hands-on experiences through learner
centered approaches.
Teachers are however, advised to explore more avenues of learner activities so as to maximise
the exploitation of skills in learners. The module is not an end in itself.
It is hoped that the module will make learning History at Ordinary Level Secondary more
meaningful and enjoyable as it is highly activity oriented.

TOPIC 1.7: NATIONAL HERITAGE SITES IN ZAMBIA
Introduction
Zambia has many heritage sites that are preserved and protected for their natural and cul-
tural importance. The sites have materials which people value, preserve and exhibit as sym-
bols of their identity. They include places where historic events occurred, buildings, objects
and natural or geographical features that are important to the society. National heritage
sites are important for business and must be preserved to boost their contribution towards
development. This topic discusses national heritage sites in Zambia. It identifies some of
the heritage sites in the country, discusses their importance and explores measures that
promote their conservation.
Hook
Our rich and varied cultural heritage has a profound power to build our nation.
(Nelson Mandela)
A concerted effort to preserve our heritage is a vital link to our cultural, educational,
aesthetic, inspirational and economic legacies – all of the things that quite literary
make us who we are. (Steve Berry)

KEY WORDS
Heritage: resources which people inherit from past generations and consider to
be valuable and worth preserving as symbols of their identity.
Site: a place of historical or cultural importance.
Monument: a structure that is erected in memory of an event or a person.
National heritage sites: places that have resources of historical and cultural
importance to the identity of the nation.
SUB-TOPIC 1.7.1: HERITAGE SITES IN ZAMBIA
Zambia has more than four thousand heritage sites spread around the ten prov-
inces. These include monuments, natural sites, historical sites and cultural sites.
Heritage sites have been recognised by the government and are protected under
the Laws of Zambia. This sub-topic focuses on national heritage sites in Zambia.
It identifies heritage sites in various parts of the country and describes the ways
of promoting them.

CONTENT TIPS
Some of the heritage sites in Zambia are as follows:
Zambezi River Source
This site is located in Ikelenge District of North-Western Province. It has a cold water
spring which forms the source of the Zambezi River. The Zambezi River Source is
portrayed as the birthplace of Zambia. Water from the spring is believed to have
healing power. The local people treat the site as a sacred place and do not allow
cutting of trees in the area.
Chirundu Fossil Forest
This site is located in Chirundu District. The site has several fossils of tree trunks
estimated to be about 150 million years old.
Barotse Cultural Landscape
This site is located in the Zambezi Flood Plain in Western Province. It is a vast land
containing a network of canals built by the Lozi people for flood control,
transportation and irrigation. The site also has several royal burial sites and royal
villages.
Mwela Rocks
This site is located in the forest surrounding Kasama town in Northern Province. It has
paintings attributed to the Late Stone Age.
Thandwe Rock Shelter
This site is located in the Thandwe Hills in Chipata District. It has evidence of human
occupation by Late Stone Age and Iron Age people.

CONTENT TIPS
 The Mosi-O-Tunya (Victoria Falls) is one of Zambia’s heritage sites. It has the
largest curtain of falling water in the world.
 The Victoria Falls is one of the early human settlements in Zambia. The site
has remains of stone tools such as axes, knives, spearheads and arrowheads
belonging to the Stone Age people. These tools demonstrate that early
humans lived in the area from around three million years ago.
 The local people known as the Toka-Leya named the falls as Mosi-O-Tunya
which means the smoke that thunders.
 The Scottish missionary explorer, David Livingstone visited the falls in 1855
and named them Victoria Falls after Queen Victoria of England. The Mosi-
O-Tunya is important for tourism.

CONTENT TIPS
Traditional ceremonies in Zambia
Shimunenga of the Ila
The Shimunenga is a traditional ceremony of the Ila people of Namwala District
held annually in September or October. The Ila hold the ceremony to honour
their legendary warrior Shimunenga and leader. They also celebrate their history
and cattle-keeping tradition, which is central to their identity and livelihood. The
event features parades of decorated cattle, traditional songs, and dances that
honour the spirits of their ancestors. Rituals are performed by the Kafue River,
symbolising the Ila people’s harmonious relationship with nature. This ceremony
is an opportunity for the Ila to pass down cultural values and knowledge to
younger generations.
Ukusefya pa Ng’wena of the Bemba
Ukusefya pa Ng’wena is a traditional ceremony of the Bemba people presided
over by Paramount Chief Chitimukulu. The ceremony is held in Mungwi district in
Northern Province around August or September at Ng’wena village. It is held to
celebrate the origin, formation of the Bemba ethnic group and their
achievements. During the ceremony, traditional dances, drumming, and
speeches by leaders highlight the event, while storytelling serves as a means
of preserving Bemba history. The ceremony also emphasises the importance of
unity and identity among the Bemba people.
Kulamba of the Chewa
Kulamba is the traditional ceremony of the Chewa people of Malawi, Zambia and
Mozambique. Kulamba is a time the Chewa people honour their ancestors and
pay respect to their paramount Chief, Kalonga Gawa Undi, both present and past.
It is held annually in August in Katete and is hosted by His Majesty Kalonga Gawa
Undi at Mkaika palace.

Lwindi Gonde of the Tonga
The Lwindi Gonde is an annual traditional ceremony of the Tonga people of
Southern Province. The ceremony takes place at Gonde in July near Chief Monze’s
palace in Monze District. It is a thanks giving ceremony held at malende shrine to
appease the gods for the good rains and harvest. The ceremony involves making
offerings to ancestral spirits, performing traditional dances, drumming, and
storytelling which highlights the community’s connection to the land and their
ancestors.
Umutomboko of the Lunda
Umutomboko is a traditional ceremony of the Lunda people of Luapula. It is a
celebration of their migration from Katanga to the Luapula Valley. The ceremony
takes place annually in Mwansabombwe in July. The event is marked by the
mutomboko dance, which symbolises their triumph and resilience. Traditional
music, feasting, and rituals are also performed, symbolising the Lunda culture.
Nc’wala of the Ngoni
Nc’wala is a traditional ceremony celebrated by the Ngoni people of Eastern
Province. The ceremony is held annually in February in Mtenguleni Village, Chipata
District. It is held to honour their chief and ancestral spirits. The ceremony also
marks the first harvest of the year. Paramount Chief Mpezeni leads the ceremony,
which includes the symbolic tasting of fresh produce, the ritualistic slaughter of
a bull and drinking of its blood. Traditional dances and war songs depict Ngoni
people’s military heritage.
Kuomboka of the Lozi
The Kuomboka (to get out of the water) is a traditional ceremony of the Lozi people
of Western Province which takes place at the end of the rainy season in March or
April depending on the level of flooding, food availability and phase of the moon.
The Litunga (king) of the Lozi people move from his summer home of Lealui to his
winter home of Limulunga to avoid floods. It features a grand procession led by
the royal barge, Nalikwanda, moved by paddlers clad in traditional attire. It also
features traditional dances and music from royal drummers.
Likumbi Lya Mize of the Luvale
Likumbi Lya Mize is an annual traditional ceremony of the Luvale people of Zam-
bezi District in North Western Province. The ceremony takes place in August on
the Zambezi River. The ceremony marks the end of Mukanda, an initiation rite
for boys transitioning into adulthood. The ceremony involves the appearance of
makishi dancers who wear elaborate masks representing ancestral spirits.

SUMMARY

backgrounds, allowing them to learn about and appreciate one another’s

expressing shared values. The topic emphasises that traditional ceremonies not
only celebrate cultural identities but also play a crucial role in enhancing social
cohesion and fostering mutual respect among Zambia’s diverse communities.
