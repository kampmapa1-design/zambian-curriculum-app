classroom dynamics and to explore alternative, acceptable approaches when and where necessary.

new  competence-based  curriculum,  enhancing  their  ability  to  inspire  and  equip  learners  with
essential competences, knowledge, skills, values and attitude are aimed at enabling learners to
live and grow into productive and useful members of their communities and the Zambian society.

1.8 RISK MANAGEMENT
INTRODUCTION
Risk management is key for the successful delivery of objectives for every organisation. Risks
are  unforeseen  circumstances  that  may  have  an  adverse  effect  on  people  as  well  as  business
entities. Risk management refers to the process that individuals and organisations use to identify,
assess  and  mitigate  uncertainties  and  potential  losses  of  business,  assets  and  reputation.  Such
uncertainties  can  be  managed  by  using  certain  methods.  This  topic  has  one  sub-  topic:  The
concept of risk management. The topic will help learners to be able to assess and handle potential
risks in life.
General Competences
Problem Solving: Learners to identify analyse and apply risk management strategies.
Financial Education: Learners to demonstrate understanding of risk management.

an individual’s or organisation’s well-being.

A Business Plan
A  business  plan  is  a  written  document  that  outlines  the  goals,  strategies,  and
activities  of  a  new  or  existing  business.  It  is  used  to  conduct  market  research,
analyse competitors, validate ideas, and attract investors.
The functions of a business plan are;
 5To define the short-term and long-term goals of a business and how to achieve
them.
 5To identify the costs, sources of funding and expected income of a business.
 5To describe the core business activities and the market opportunity of a business.
 5To attract outside investors and secure financing for a business.
 5To guide the executive team and keep them focused on the business strategy.
Writing  a  business  plan  is  a  critical  step  in  starting  and  managing  your  business.
Here is an overview of the process:
Executive  Summary:  a  brief  overview  of
your  business,  including  your
mission statement, the products or services you offer, and your basic financial
information.
Company  Description:  Provide  detailed  information  about  your  company,
including its history, structure, and the problems it solves.
Market  Analysis:  Research  your  industry,  market  size,  and  competitors  to
understand your position and potential for growth.
Organisation  and  Management:  Outline  your  company’s  organisational
structure, details about the ownership, and profiles of your management team.
Service or Product Line: Describe what you sell or what service you provide.
Explain how it benefits customers and what makes it unique

Types of business organisation
Sole trader or
Sole Proprietorship
A sole trader is the sole owner of the business, meaning an individual has full control
over decision-making over the business.
Cooperative
A  cooperative  is  a  business  model  owned  and  democratically  controlled  by  its
members,  who  are  typically  customers,  employees, or  suppliers.  It  is  a  collective
enterprise that operates for the mutual benefit of its members rather than for profit
maximisation.
Partnership: A business owned by two or more individuals who share profits and
responsibilities.
Corporation – A separate legal entity owned by shareholders. It provides liability
protection for owners but comes with more regulations and taxes.
Limited Liability Company (LLC) – A hybrid structure that combines features of
a corporation and a partnership. Owners get liability protection while maintaining
operational flexibility.
Franchise – A business model where an individual operates under the branding and
structure of a larger company in exchange for fees or royalties.
